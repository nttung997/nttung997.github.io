1. Seven ships, and a strike plan that is not yet an order

The IRGC said Thursday that four vessels transferring oil in the northern Gulf were hit and caught fire, and that three more struck mines west of Hormuz. No flags or casualties were given, and the claim is not independently verified. Trump called the hits fabricated to lift the oil price before the midterms. A US Central Command spokesperson told Al Jazeera the mine claim was wrong, the lanes had been cleared, and about 95% of vessels were getting through.

The New York Times reported, and Israeli officials told ABC, that the two militaries have plans for renewed strikes after the 3 November vote, including petrochemical sites and the buried nuclear site at Pickaxe Mountain. Trump has not approved them. The White House said the United States will not attack Iran before election day.

Reuters reported that Aramco told staff to leave its Dhahran headquarters, and that a Houthi strike on Dammam airport killed two people and wounded eight before the airport reopened. The State Department ordered non-essential staff and families out of Saudi Arabia. Oil was at its highest since May, and the Saudi index fell 2.5%.

Sources: Al Jazeera, ABC, Reuters

2. Sixteen dead after a magnitude-7.0 quake in Baqen

The quake hit Baqen County, Nagqu, at 22:33 Thursday, 10 km deep according to China's earthquake network. The USGS put the epicenter about 190 km east-northeast of Nagqu. As of 2:25 a.m. Friday, the city emergency bureau reported 16 dead, 32 injured, 11 of them serious, and 339 homes collapsed. Tibet opened a Level-II response at 23:00. The count was still being checked.

Sources: Xinhua, Ifeng / Xinhua photos

3. A state land-price table, and 5% held back on a sale

Thursday morning the Assembly heard three drafts: Land, Housing, and Real Estate Business. They are not law. The land draft drops the “specific land price” and the outside valuation firms. The state would set prices from the official table and an adjustment coefficient, using land data, and is told to balance the state, users and investors. The temporary decrees that fill the gaps expire on 28 February 2027, so the new law has to be issued before 1 March.

The housing draft adds a chapter on rental housing, shifts 20 of 21 procedures to local government, and cuts three-quarters of the business conditions. The real-estate draft secures the last 5% of a contract in a blocked account, or on terms the parties agree.

Sources: Đại Đoàn Kết, chinhphu.vn

4. PHP2Shell is in the language, not the plugin

PHP patched two critical flaws on 21 October, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, both scored 9.2. Someone who never logs in can run code on a vulnerable server, and can also read secrets in that server's memory. Releases from 5.4 through 8.5.11 are affected. The fixed builds are 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35. PHP 8.1 and older will not be patched.

WordPress, Drupal and Joomla were the demonstrations. The bug sits under the application. Cloudflare was already seeing attack traffic as the patches went public, and on 22 October AWS added a firewall rule. Wiz says frontier models made a decade-old bug practical to exploit. Upgrading PHP is the only complete fix.

Sources: Wiz, Cloudflare, AWS, updated 22 Oct

5. Hanoi fair this weekend: use the Campus France desk

BMI and Times Higher Education, Pullman Hanoi, 40 Cát Linh, 13:30–17:00 on 24 and 25 October. Campus France is in the French pavilion. The busiest French stands are private schools. For an English-track public licence the useful conversation is Paris 1's international economics track, AMU IPEM, and UPEC. An IELTS 7.0 score is already above the floors those programmes publish, so the questions are maths, the Études en France file, and whether the programme really is the public licence.

NTHU is on the organiser's expected Taiwan list. Its English business round closes at noon on 23 November. NTU is not on the list. Eiffel and France Excellence are master's awards, not a bachelor application.
