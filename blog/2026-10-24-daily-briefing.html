Daily briefing: 24 October 2026

24 Oct 2026 · news from 23 October
  • Israeli forces cordoned Hebron after gunmen killed two soldiers. Israel votes on Tuesday.

1. Hebron cordoned after two soldiers are killed

Gunmen killed Staff Sgt. Ron Yonatan Roytman and Sgt. Yuval Shai Svilem near Hebron on Friday and wounded three other soldiers. Troops killed the two attackers. Netanyahu ordered the city cordoned off and said forces would evacuate the neighbourhood the attackers came from, search house to house, and destroy what he called terrorist infrastructure.

A Reuters reporter saw bulldozers start demolishing nearby homes. Residents said those homes were not the attackers'. The military did not immediately comment. Israel votes on Tuesday.

Sources: Al Jazeera, CBS, Reuters

2. Moody's leaves France at Aa3

Friday's periodic review left the rating at Aa3 and the negative outlook unchanged. Markets had been positioned for a cut that would have matched S&P and Fitch. Moody's said Aa3 assumes a deficit-reducing 2027 budget is adopted and that borrowing costs and social tensions ease somewhat.

The finance ministry tied the hold to the 2027 finance bill. Prime Minister Lecornu said the path is a budget by year-end consistent with a deficit of 5% of GDP.

Sources: CNBC, Franceinfo, Reuters

3. US banks block third-party AI agents

Citi, JPMorgan, Bank of America and Wells Fargo are blocking outside AI agents from logging into customer accounts or moving money. Citi said it does not allow those agents to log in or transact, and that a customer may authorize only limited read-only access. Morgan Stanley has also blocked third-party agents and, as of this reporting, had not seen a rogue agent.

Sources: USA Today, Reuters

4. Hanoi drafts wider access for foreign investors

On Friday the finance minister put draft amendments to the Law on Investment and a revised Bidding Law before the National Assembly. Neither has been passed. The investment draft would add support for supply chains, high-tech manufacturing, fixed assets, and cooperation with universities and research institutes. It would also let the government ease foreign market-access conditions, after asking the Standing Committee where the restriction sits in a law.

Phan Văn Mãi said wider access is the right direction, but that this new power needs a hard look and that incentives should be tied to results. The bidding draft would give project owners more choice of contractor method. The committee said open bidding should remain the default.

Sources: Vietnam News, Vietnam News, bidding draft
