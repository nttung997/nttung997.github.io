AI since GitHub Copilot

23 Oct 2026
  • 29 June 2021: Copilot’s technical preview put a code model inside Visual Studio Code.
  • 30 November 2022: ChatGPT made the same kind of model a consumer product. It still could not search the web.
  • 7 December 2022: Perplexity was the first public AI that answered from live search, with citations.
  • 12 September 2024: o1 spent extra compute thinking. 20 January 2025: DeepSeek-R1 showed that trick could be trained cheaply, in the open.
  • By October 2026 the flagship is a monthly release, and the product people keep open is the agent around the model.

The pair programmer

On 29 June 2021 GitHub opened a technical preview of Copilot in Visual Studio Code. It was built with OpenAI on Codex, a code-heavy descendant of GPT-3, and it suggested whole lines and functions from the file you had open. Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Ruby, and Go worked best. OpenAI shipped the Codex API on 10 August 2021.

Copilot left preview on 21 June 2022 at $10 a month, or $100 a year, and stayed free for verified students and maintainers of popular open-source projects. That price made generative AI an ordinary software subscription about five months before most people had heard of a chatbot.

The same year produced the rest of the generative boom. InstructGPT, in January, showed that human feedback could make a model follow instructions. DALL·E 2 in April, and the open release of Stable Diffusion on 22 August, did for images what Copilot had done for code. On 30 November OpenAI put a GPT-3.5 chat box on the public internet. ChatGPT reached about 100 million users in two months.

Sources: GitHub, 29 Jun 2021, GitHub changelog, 21 Jun 2022

Everyone ships a product

A blank text box needed no editor. By early 2023 Microsoft had put a model stronger than the public ChatGPT into Bing, announced on 7 February. GPT-4 and Anthropic’s Claude both appeared on 14 March. Google opened Bard on 21 March.

GPT-4 was the capability step: more reliable on professional tasks, and immediately the model other labs were measured against. Eight days later GitHub announced Copilot X: chat and voice, pull-request help, docs Q&A, and a command-line tool, on GPT-4. Autocomplete was now the smallest surface of the coding product.

Meta’s LLaMA, released to researchers on 24 February and then widely leaked, started the open-weight counterweight. Llama 2 followed on 18 July with a license businesses could actually use. Mistral 7B in September showed a small open model could be startlingly good. OpenAI’s DevDay on 6 November shipped GPT-4 Turbo, custom GPTs, and the Assistants API. On 17 November the board fired Sam Altman. The decision to bring him back came on 21 November. In December Google announced Gemini.

Sources: Microsoft, 7 Feb 2023, GitHub, 22 Mar 2023, OpenAI, 29 Nov 2023

Long context, then a model that pauses

Early 2024 was a multimodal sprint. OpenAI previewed Sora on 15 February, generated video that held a shot. Google’s Gemini 1.5 Pro, the same month, made a million-token context the headline: a whole codebase, or a long video, in one prompt. Claude 3 on 4 March and Llama 3 on 18 April reset the quality of the second and third models. GPT-4o on 13 May folded text, vision, and speech into one real-time model.

Claude 3.5 Sonnet, on 20 June, mattered more for daily work than its place on a chart. It was strong at coding, cheaper than the previous flagship, and it shipped with Artifacts, a pane where the model’s output was something you could edit. In October an upgraded Sonnet could operate a desktop. Cognition’s Devin, demoed on 12 March, had already sold the idea of an “AI software engineer.” The demos ran ahead of the reliability.

On 12 September OpenAI released o1-preview, the first widely used model trained to spend extra compute before it answered. That was the template for 2025. Llama 3.1 in July put an open model at frontier scale. The EU AI Act entered into force on 1 August. By December, Gemini 2.0 and DeepSeek-V3 were already pointed at agents and at cost.

Reasoning gets cheap

DeepSeek-R1, released 20 January 2025, was an open-weight reasoning model in the neighborhood of o1, trained for a small fraction of what Western labs were spending. On 27 January Nvidia fell as much as 17 percent in a day, reported at the time as the largest single-day loss of market value for a US company, and the DeepSeek app passed ChatGPT on the US App Store.

The product form changed with the price. On 2 February Andrej Karpathy called the new habit “vibe coding”: describing software in language and letting the model write it. On 24 February Anthropic shipped Claude 3.7 Sonnet, which could answer quickly or think at length, and Claude Code, a terminal agent for engineering work. Through the spring, Gemini 2.5 Pro (25 March), Llama 4 (5 April), o3 (16 April), and Claude 4 (22 May) all assumed tool use and longer jobs. GPT-5 arrived on 7 August with a fast mode and a thinking mode behind one router, and a rocky first week.

The year also produced the first hard public limits. METR’s July trial found 16 experienced open-source developers were 19 percent slower on 246 real tasks in their own repositories when they could use early-2025 AI tools, after predicting they would be faster. In June, Judge William Alsup held that training on lawfully acquired books can be fair use and that keeping a library of pirated copies is not. Anthropic later agreed to pay at least $1.5 billion to settle that class action. By November the flagship tier had already moved on to GPT-5.1, Gemini 3 Pro, and Claude Opus 4.5.

Sources: 2025 in artificial intelligence

A flagship a month

Through the first half of 2026 the version numbers stopped meaning a new era. OpenAI moved through GPT-5.3 Codex, GPT-5.4, and GPT-5.5. Anthropic walked Opus from 4.6 to 4.8 and opened a tier above it. Mythos stayed with vetted partners. Fable 5, in June, was the generally available model in that class. Google’s Gemini 3.1 Pro landed in February. Meta, after Llama 4’s reception and Yann LeCun’s departure at the end of 2025, shipped Muse Spark in April, its first closed frontier model.

The summer compressed a generation into weeks. GPT-5.6 reached general availability on 9 July. Kimi K3, the same month, was the largest open-weight release to that date. On 1 September Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.1. On 4 September OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra, then GPT-6 Sol and Luna on 22 September, the same day as Claude Opus 5.5. xAI’s Grok 4.7 arrived the day before.

October kept the pace. Meta’s Muse Wonder opened on 12 October. Gemini 4 Argon, restricted at the end of September, became generally available on 13 October. DeepSeek posted a new V4.1 Flash checkpoint on 17 October. A lab that had shipped one flagship a year in 2023 was shipping one a month. Coding products treated the model as swappable. The surface people kept open was the agent that could read a repository, run commands, and stay on a task.

Sources: Frontier release log, checked through 21 Oct 2026

When web search showed up

Copilot and the first ChatGPT answered from training data. Copilot saw the file you had open. ChatGPT’s knowledge stopped in 2021. Live web search is a separate feature, and it arrived in public products a week after ChatGPT, from a startup rather than from OpenAI.

On 7 December 2022 Perplexity opened a search box with no login. It sent the question to Bing, then used GPT-3.5 to summarize the results and attach citations. That is the first date a member of the public could ask a language model a question and get an answer grounded in that day’s web.

The first big-lab version was Microsoft’s new Bing, launched in preview on 7 February 2023. Search, chat, and citations were the product. Bard, opened on 21 March, was built as a companion to Google Search: a “Google it” button sent you to ordinary results, and the model itself rarely cited a page. Google’s AI answers inside Search, the Search Generative Experience, started as a labs experiment in May 2023.

ChatGPT itself got a browsing beta for Plus subscribers on 12 May 2023. Browse with Bing rolled out from 24 May, was switched off in early July after people used it to get past paywalls, and came back on 27 September for Plus and Enterprise. On 31 October 2024 OpenAI shipped ChatGPT Search, which decides on its own when to look something up. It reached every logged-in user on 16 December 2024, and anyone without an account on 5 February 2025.

Claude waited longer. Web search opened on 20 March 2025 as a preview for paid users in the United States, on Claude 3.7 Sonnet. It went to every plan worldwide on 27 May 2025.

Sources: Perplexity on Hacker News, 7 Dec 2022, Microsoft Bing, 7 Feb 2023, Search Engine Land, on Bard, Reuters, 27 Sep 2023, OpenAI, 31 Oct 2024, Anthropic, 20 Mar 2025

Where it stands

On 23 October 2026 the frontier is a set of interchangeable models: Claude Fable and Opus 5.5, GPT-6, Gemini 4 Argon, Grok 4.7, Muse Wonder, DeepSeek V4. They sit under agents that read repositories and use tools. Open weights from DeepSeek, Moonshot, Zhipu, Alibaba, and Mistral are close enough, and cheap enough, that the closed labs no longer hold the only usable copy of the technique.

GitHub’s early studies found developers finishing a canned task faster with Copilot. In July 2025, METR measured the opposite on real work: experienced developers using early-2025 tools took longer on repositories they already knew, and still believed they had sped up. The argument has moved from whether a model can write a function to how long an agent can work before it drifts, who is liable when it is wrong, and which lab can pay for the next training run. The coding product that started the period is still the clearest place to watch it.
